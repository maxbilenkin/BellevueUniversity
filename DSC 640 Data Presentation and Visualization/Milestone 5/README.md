# DSC 640 – Data Presentation and Visualization

## Course Overview
Data scientists must be effective storytellers, communicating insights clearly through visualizations, written explanations, and presentations. This course focuses on the principles of data storytelling and how different types of visualizations can be used to communicate insights to different audiences. Students learn how to structure a narrative around data, design effective visualizations, and present findings in ways that support decision-making.

## Project: Rising Infant Childcare Costs in the United States

### Project Description
This project explores trends in infant childcare costs in the United States using the **National Database of Childcare Prices**. The analysis focuses on how the median price of center-based infant childcare has changed over time and what those trends may mean for families and policymakers.

Childcare is one of the largest expenses faced by young families. Rising costs can place financial pressure on households and may influence decisions about family planning, workforce participation, and long-term economic stability.

The goal of this project is to analyze childcare cost trends and present the findings through clear visual storytelling.

### Dataset
The dataset used for this project contains **34,566 rows and 227 columns** and includes childcare pricing information across U.S. states and counties over multiple years.

The analysis focuses on the **MCInfant** variable, which represents the **median price of center-based infant childcare**.

### Key Findings
The analysis shows that infant childcare prices increased substantially between **2008 and 2018**.

- In **2008**, the median price was **$120.64**
- By **2018**, the median price increased to **$153.60**
- This represents an increase of **more than 27% over the decade**

Price increases did not occur evenly each year. Some years experienced faster growth, while others showed small declines. However, the overall trend clearly indicates that childcare costs have been rising over time.

These findings highlight the financial burden childcare can place on families, particularly younger parents and households living in high-cost areas.

### Data Visualization Techniques
The project uses several visualization methods to communicate the data story:

- **Line Chart** – to show the trend in median childcare prices over time  
- **Clustered Bar Chart** – to compare childcare costs across categories  
- **Microsoft Azure Map** – to visualize geographic differences in childcare costs  

These visualizations help illustrate both **temporal trends** and **regional patterns** in childcare pricing.

### Target Audience
The primary audience for this analysis includes:

- Policymakers  
- Childcare advocacy organizations  
- Community organizations supporting families and early-childhood development  

These groups can use the insights from the analysis to better understand childcare affordability challenges and potential policy responses.

### Communication Mediums
The project communicates findings through several formats:

- **PowerPoint presentation** with charts and key insights  
- **Interactive visualizations** (potentially using Tableau or Power BI)  
- **One-page policy brief** summarizing the main findings and recommendations  

These formats allow the analysis to reach both technical and non-technical audiences.

### Policy Implications
The steady increase in childcare costs suggests that many families face growing financial pressure when raising children. Rising childcare expenses may contribute to declining birth rates and affect long-term economic growth.

Potential policy responses may include:

- Increasing funding for childcare programs  
- Expanding the number of childcare centers  
- Supporting initiatives that improve childcare affordability  

Improving access to affordable childcare could reduce financial stress for families while supporting workforce participation and economic stability.
